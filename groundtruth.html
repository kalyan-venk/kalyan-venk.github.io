GroundTruth

An A/B analysis that runs its validity checks before it computes the effect, and reports the ones that fire. 13.9M rows of a Criteo uplift log through Spark, six guardrails, CUPED variance reduction, always-valid sequential testing, and a ship recommendation with the caveats attached. Three of the guardrails fire on this data, and the number moves by a factor of two depending on how you treat 1.26M duplicate rows. The ship decision survives all of it. The precise number does not.

The recommendation

Ship it, and target it.

Conversion rises 47.4% in relative terms once the covariate imbalance is adjusted out. The bar for "worth shipping" was set at a 10% relative lift before any effect was computed, and the weakest lower bound across every specification tried is +38.6%, so the decision holds however the data is handled. Then targeting: 95% of the incremental conversions come from the top 30% of users by predicted uplift, so roughly 70% of the ad spend could go without losing much.

The number is honest about its own softness. This log is a constructed 85/15 split, not a clean randomised experiment. The arms differ on every observed pre-assignment feature, and the measured magnitude moves by 2x depending on how 1.26M duplicate rows are treated. The direction and the ship call survive all of that. No adjustment fixes imbalance on features nobody recorded, so the precise number is reported with a plausible range of +42.1% to +85.4%, not a single confident figure.

The stats layer never sees a row

Spark reduces 13.9M rows to per-arm sums and the driver receives a 68 KB parquet file. Every test here (z-test, CUPED, Hotelling T²) is additive, so it runs off those sums. Full run: about 80 seconds.

Guardrails first, on purpose

Three of six fire: the split is under-dispersed (ratio is exactly 17:3), the file is not shuffled (13% to 100% treatment by block), and all 12 features differ. The effect is only computed after they run.

CUPED earns its place on robustness

The unadjusted lift spans 1.44x across three duplicate-handling specs, the adjusted one spans 1.12x. Adjustment cuts specification sensitivity 3.5x. That, not the 10.7% variance reduction, is the case for CUPED here.

Sequential decision · if you peeked

always-valid interval is wider than fixed-horizon

The CUPED bar is the cyan one. Variance falls by ρ², so the interval, which scales with the square root of variance, falls by 1 minus the square root of (1 minus ρ²): a smaller number, and quoting the variance figure as an interval improvement would nearly double the claim. On the Criteo log the covariate correlates at 0.34, giving an 11.8% theoretical variance reduction and 10.7% actually achieved.

What each guardrail catches

Six checks run before any effect is computed

They catch different failures. On this dataset three of them fire, and the SRM pass is itself a finding.

CheckAsksResult here
SRMdo the arms have the right number of users?pass suspiciously well
Under-dispersionis the split too close to design?fires ratio is exactly 17:3
Row-orderis assignment correlated with file position?fires 13% to 100% by block
Balancedo the arms hold the same kind of user?fires all 12 features differ
Specification curvedoes the answer depend on a cleaning choice?fires 1.44x spread
Peekingwould early looks have inflated the error rate?28.8% at 30 looks
The perfect SRM pass is the problem. The split is 85.00/15.00, off by 2 users out of 13,979,592, at chi-square p = 0.9989. Random assignment lands that close 0.11% of the time, and the arm ratio agrees with 17/3 to seven significant figures. Control was downsampled to hit exactly 15%, so the pass shows the guardrail runs, not that the upstream randomiser is sound.
The duplicate rows decide the magnitude. 2.22M rows sit in byte-identical groups with no user ID to disambiguate them. They carry zero conversions across 2.22M rows where outcome-independent duplication predicts about 7,700, which is why all rows are kept. Raw lift runs +59.45% / +73.38% / +85.37% across the three readings; the CUPED-adjusted lift barely moves.

What I got wrong

An external review caught three wrong numbers in the shipped headline

The first version reported a CUPED lift of +50.76%, an adjusted numerator over an unadjusted control mean, which is exactly the denominator CUPED says you cannot use. The consistent figure is +47.36%. The relative confidence intervals were 1.49x too narrow because they divided by a fixed control mean, and control is the small arm in an 85/15 split. The CACE was built on the wrong ITT. All three are corrected, and there is a regression test in tests/test_stats.py for each so they cannot come back. The corrections are the most useful part of the repo, and MISTAKES.md has the longer list.
